# Praxis Support

Praxis is a habit and practice tracker for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, built by Matt Peterson. If something's broken, confusing, or you have an idea, this is where to start.

Praxis 2.0 needs iOS or iPadOS 17 or later. The Apple Watch app needs watchOS 10 or later.

Some older devices can't update to iOS 17: iPhone 8, iPhone 8 Plus, iPhone X, iPad (5th generation), and the 9.7-inch and first 12.9-inch iPad Pro. If you already use Praxis on one of them, you keep Praxis 1.5. Someone who hasn't downloaded Praxis before can't install it on those devices. If your iPhone or iPad can run iOS 17 but hasn't updated yet, update iOS to get Praxis 2.0.

## Core concepts

A few ideas show up everywhere in Praxis. If you understand these, the rest of the app falls into place quickly.

### Practices

A **practice** is anything you want to track over time. A workout, a journal entry, watering the plants, calling your mom, twenty pages of a book. Praxis treats them all the same way: each one has a name, a place in your life, and a sense of how often you want to do it.

Add one from the **Practices** tab → tap **+ Add**, then pick a template or start from scratch. To change a practice later, tap it in the **Practices** tab to open its details. On **Focus**, you can also swipe a practice to reveal **Edit** and **Stats**.

The vocabulary is deliberately broad. "Practice" comes from Aristotle's idea of *praxis*: purposeful action that becomes part of who you are when repeated. Whether you call them habits, rituals, routines, or jobs around the house, they're all practices in this sense.

### Cadence: target, min, and max

When you create a practice, Praxis asks how often you want to do it. You can give it up to three numbers (in days):

- **Target frequency** is the rhythm you're aiming for. *"Run every 3 days."*
- **Minimum frequency** is the floor. Before this many days have passed, Praxis marks the practice "Too soon." *"No sooner than every 2 days."*
- **Maximum frequency** is the ceiling. Past this, the practice becomes "Must do." *"Even at the slowest, every 10 days."*

The three numbers create a band, not a single line. A practice doesn't have to land on its target every time to count as on-track. Life gets in the way; cadence accounts for that.

Only the target is required. If you leave min and max blank, Praxis simply scores the practice against its target.

### Days, reminders, and due dates

A few optional settings in a practice's details make the schedule fit real life:

- **Applies on** lets you pick the days a practice counts. Weekday-only practices wait under **Not today** on Focus during the weekend, and the days off don't count against them. A practice last done Friday reads as due on Monday, not three days overdue.
- **Reminder** sends a notification at a time you choose on the day the practice is due. It skips days you've already done it and days it doesn't apply.
- **Next due date** is for things tied to the calendar, like trash day. Praxis keeps the practice on a fixed schedule from that date, so logging it a day early doesn't shift the rhythm.

### Scoring: where each practice stands

Every practice gets a status based on when you last did it, compared with its cadence:

- **On track** and **Due soon**: you're keeping rhythm.
- **Due today**: today is the day.
- **Overdue** and **Very overdue**: past the target, and further past it.
- **Must do**: past the maximum you set.
- **Too soon**: you did it recently, inside the minimum.
- **In rhythm**: for practices with a daily count or time goal, you're still showing up, just below target.
- **Progress like "12/20 today"**: partway through today's count or minutes.
- **Not today**: the practice doesn't apply on this day of the week.

These show up as colors throughout Focus, and how they look depends on your design style: **Classic** puts a colored bar on each card, **Aura** washes the card with color, **Pill** adds a top border and a status badge, and **Minimal** uses plain text rows. Pick whichever reads best to you under **Settings → Design Style**, and choose a color theme under **Settings → Appearance**. (Open the menu with the button in the top-right corner to find **Settings**.)

The scoring is about that one practice. Being overdue on one thing doesn't mark down everything else. It's just information.

### Life areas, zones, and rooms

Most habit apps organize practices in a flat list. Praxis lets you give them a place, both physically and metaphorically.

- **Life areas** are the top level. *Home, Health, Relationships, Creative.*
- **Zones** are optional groupings inside an area. *Home → Upstairs, Outdoors.*
- **Rooms** are specific places inside an area, optionally within a zone. *Home → Kitchen, Bedroom.*

Manage them from the **Areas** tab: **+ Life Area**, **+ Room**, and **Zones**. You're not required to use any of this. But once you start grouping practices, the app becomes more useful: Focus can filter by area, zone or room, Plan helps you batch related work, and Stats shows whether the **Yard** is keeping pace with the **Kitchen**.

The framing comes from a simple observation: your life has shape. The kitchen is different from the home office, and a Tuesday morning is different from a Saturday afternoon. Praxis lets you reflect that shape in how you track yourself.

### Colors

Each color in Praxis has one job:

- **Your theme's color shows today's progress.** The ring you see after logging a practice fills toward today's total: the practices due today or overdue, plus what you've done today. The Focus header, the Today's Progress widget and the Apple Watch ring all count today the same way. An unfinished ring stays that calm color, never red, and each day starts fresh.
- **Green, yellow, orange and red describe one practice's rhythm:** on track, due soon, overdue or very overdue (see [Scoring](#scoring-where-each-practice-stands)). Focus cards also say it in words, such as "Overdue" or "12/20 today".
- **Area and type colors group things.** Each life area and practice type keeps its own color, shown next to its name, on the Focus filter chips and in Stats. A practice uses its area's color, and Home has one of its own.

To choose a color, open the **Areas** tab and tap **Edit** on an area, or open a practice and tap **Type settings** under **Practice Type**. Pick one of eight colors, or leave it on **Auto**, which picks a calm color for you and keeps it when you add or reorder areas.

### Insights: what Praxis notices

Once a week, Praxis looks for patterns in your completion history, on your device. Things like:

- *You tend to do A and B on the same day.*
- *Your sessions of this practice have been getting longer.*
- *One life area has been quiet for three weeks or more.*
- *You've settled at a different daily count than your target, so maybe update the target.*

Insights appear in **Stats → Insights**. New ones are marked **New**, and earlier ones stay in your history. They only appear when there's enough data to be confident: most need 6 to 8 sessions of a practice. They describe your own rhythm in plain numbers, with no grades and no streaks. When a target and your actual rhythm have drifted apart, a card suggests a target closer to how you really practice, which you can change again any time. The insight cards are free.

## Flows

A **Flow** is a chained sequence of practices that you move through in order.

Think of a morning routine. You meditate, then journal, then exercise. Each is a real practice you'd track on its own. With a Flow, you string them together: when you finish one, the next is ready to go. No tab-switching, no hunting through your list, no breaking concentration.

### How to set one up

Open the menu → **Flows** → **+**. Give the Flow a name (e.g., *Morning*). Add steps by picking practices from your library, in the order you want them. Save.

To run it, tap the Flow. A banner at the top of **Focus** shows the current step: tap **Begin** to start it, then **Next →** to log it and move on. The banner stays put, so you don't lose your place if you switch screens mid-routine.

The free version includes one Flow. The unlock removes the limit.

### Why this is useful

Routines work because they remove decision friction. Once a sequence is established, you stop choosing what to do next; you just do the next thing. That's the whole point of a Flow.

A few examples of where they help:

- **Morning or evening rituals.** Wake-up sequence, wind-down sequence, anything anchored to a part of the day.
- **Sequential workout circuits.** Warm-up → main lift → cool-down, with timers built in.
- **Mise-en-place.** Setting up a workspace or kitchen before deeper work.
- **Multi-step housework.** Strip the bed → wash the sheets → remake. Each is a real practice; together they're a routine.

Flows aren't about doing more. They're about not having to think about how to start. The decision was already made when you built the Flow.

## Links

A **link** is a scheduling constraint between two practices. There are two kinds.

### Sequential links

*"Do A before B, within N days."*

Example: *Foam Roll → Run, within 1 day.* Praxis won't surface "Run" as a primary suggestion until "Foam Roll" has been done recently. If the link is broken, "Run" gets a small inline hint: *Do Foam Roll first.*

Use this when one practice meaningfully prepares for another. Stretching before running. Prepping a recipe before cooking it. Reading a chapter before discussing it.

### Spaced links

*"Wait at least N days between A and B."*

Example: *Heavy Cardio → Heavy Cardio, 2 days.* Praxis will block the second occurrence until enough time has passed.

Use this when two practices need recovery between them. Two leg workouts. Two long meditations. Two intense client sessions.

### Setting up a link

Open the practice that should depend on another. In its details, scroll to **Links** and tap **+ Add Link**. Pick the other practice, choose the type (sequential or spaced), and set the window in days.

### Why this matters

Most habit apps treat practices as independent. They're not. Real life has dependencies: one thing makes another easier; some things shouldn't share a day. Links let Praxis know about those dependencies and respect them when it suggests what to do next.

You don't have to use links. Most practices don't need them. But when two practices clearly belong in a particular relationship, the app should know, and now it can.

## iCloud sync

iCloud sync keeps your practices in step between your iPhone and iPad. It uses your own iCloud account. There's still no Praxis account and no Praxis server.

### Turning it on

Open the menu → **Data & Storage** → **iCloud Sync** → turn on **Sync across devices**. Do the same on each device signed in to the same Apple ID.

Sync is part of the one-time unlock. If you haven't unlocked yet, the switch opens the unlock screen first, and afterwards Praxis asks whether you'd like to turn sync on.

What syncs: your practices and completion history (including notes and ratings), life areas, zones, rooms and practice types (with any colors you picked), links, and Flows. Settings and themes stay separate on each device.

### What it needs

- Every device signed in to iCloud with the same Apple ID
- iCloud Drive turned on, with Praxis allowed to use it (**Settings → [your name] → iCloud → iCloud Drive**)
- Praxis 2.0 or later on each device

Praxis syncs when it opens and after changes. To sync right away, tap **Sync now** under the switch, or pull down on the main screens. The line under the switch shows **Syncing…** or when it last synced.

If you set up practices separately on two devices before turning on sync, the first sync combines both libraries and nothing is thrown away. If you'd built the same practice on both, you may see it twice; just delete the extra.

### "iCloud unavailable"

This shows under the switch when sync is on but Praxis can't reach iCloud Drive. Usually the device is signed out of iCloud, iCloud Drive is off, or Praxis's access to iCloud Drive has been switched off. Everything stays safe on the device in the meantime. Fix the setting, reopen Praxis, and it catches up.

### Turning it off and deleting iCloud data

Turning off **Sync across devices** stops syncing. The device keeps all of its data, and whatever is already in iCloud stays there.

To remove your Praxis data from iCloud completely:

1. Turn off **Sync across devices** on every device first, or another device will upload it again.
2. On your iPhone or iPad, go to **Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage** (called **Storage** on some iOS versions) → **Praxis**, and delete its data. Deleting the file in the Files app (**iCloud Drive → Praxis**) removes only that file, not the rest of your Praxis data in iCloud, so use Settings for a full delete.

**Reset All Data** in Data & Storage erases Praxis on that device only. It turns sync off first so an empty library can't overwrite your other devices, and it leaves your iCloud copy alone.

## Apple Watch, widgets and Live Activities

All of these are free.

### The Praxis Ring complication

Add **Praxis Ring** to a watch face to see how today is going at a glance. It comes in circular, corner, rectangular, and inline sizes.

Choose what it shows from the menu on your iPhone → **Apple Watch**:

- **Period**: today, or this week
- **Ring credit**: count only finished practices, or give partial credit for count and time goals
- **What the ring tracks**: all practices, or the ones you pick
- **List style** and **Ring name**

"Today" on the ring counts the same way as the Focus header and the Today's Progress widget: the practices due today or overdue, plus the ones you've done today. If the ring tracks a single area or type, it takes that area's or type's color.

### The watch app

Install Praxis on your watch from the Watch app on your iPhone. Tap the complication or open Praxis on your wrist to see:

- **Active**: timers running on your phone, with **Pause**/**Resume** and **Done** buttons
- **Today**: the practices the ring tracks that are due today or overdue, then the ones you've already done today, with a green check. Tap one to log it. Practices with a count or minutes goal open a quick entry you can adjust with the Digital Crown. Each row has the same status color as on your iPhone's Focus screen.

The watch lists only today's plate. A practice the ring doesn't track, or one that isn't due yet (and hasn't been done today), doesn't apply on this day of the week, or is out of season, isn't there. **Apple Watch** in the Praxis menu on your iPhone says how many practices are on your watch, and how many of the practices the ring tracks it leaves off, and why. The list holds up to 100 practices; if there are ever more than fit, it ends with *"+N more on your iPhone."*

Your iPhone does the thinking. The watch shows what the phone last sent it, and things you log on the watch are recorded by Praxis on your iPhone, so they can take a moment to appear there. If the watch says *"Open Praxis on your iPhone to feed the ring,"* do exactly that. After midnight, until Praxis has opened on your iPhone, the watch shows **New day** instead of yesterday's list. Open Praxis on your iPhone and today's list arrives.

**If Praxis won't install on your watch, or the ring stops updating.** Occasionally watchOS gets stuck installing or updating an app. It shows as "Installing" in the Watch app on your iPhone, and the ring freezes because the iPhone stops sending to a watch app it thinks is missing. **Apple Watch** in the Praxis menu will say Praxis isn't installed on your watch. To clear it:

1. Force-restart the watch: hold the side button and the Digital Crown together until the Apple logo appears.
2. Restart your iPhone.
3. In the Watch app on your iPhone, go to **My Watch → Available Apps** and tap **Install** next to Praxis. Keep the watch on its charger, unlocked and near the phone.

If it's still stuck, check **General → Software Update** in the Watch app for a pending watchOS update, then try again. Your practices and history are on your iPhone, so nothing is lost while the watch app reinstalls.

### Widgets

Praxis has nine widgets. Long-press your Home Screen, tap **+** (or **Edit → Add Widget** on iOS 18 and later), and search for Praxis.

- **Up Next**: the practices that need you today
- **Today's Progress**: how today is going
- **Life Balance**: attention across your life areas this week
- **Daily Wisdom**: a classical thought on practice, changing daily
- **Aura Focus**: your Focus list, tinted by how much each practice needs you
- **Aura Pulse**: the mood of your day, at a glance
- **Chart**: a chart of practices you choose. Set it up from the menu → **Chart widget**.
- **Quick Log**: tap **+** to log a due practice without opening the app
- **Command**: a large dashboard, for iPad only

**Up Next**, **Today's Progress**, and **Daily Wisdom** also work on the Lock Screen.

A Quick Log tap is saved straight away and recorded the next time Praxis opens, so it won't be lost even if Praxis isn't running.

### Live Activities

When you tap **Begin** on a practice, its timer appears on your Lock Screen and, on iPhones that have one, in the Dynamic Island. Each running timer gets its own Live Activity. Tap the banner to jump straight to Focus.

The **Pause**/**Resume** and **Done** buttons on the Lock Screen work through the Praxis app: a press wakes Praxis in the background, and Praxis updates the timer. That's reliable when Praxis is running in the background, which is the usual case after you lock your phone. If Praxis has been fully closed (swiped away, or shut down by iOS), a press might not register. The timer keeps running, and you can finish it in the app. After **Done**, Praxis usually asks for the session's details the next time you open it.

With iOS 18 and watchOS 11 or later, the same timer also appears in the Smart Stack on Apple Watch for a quick look, without buttons. For controls on your wrist, use the Praxis watch app.

To stop Live Activities, go to iOS **Settings → Praxis → Live Activities**.

## iPad

Praxis runs natively on iPad, in any orientation. It has the same practices, charts, timers and settings as on iPhone.

To use the same practices on both, turn on [iCloud sync](#icloud-sync) on each. The **Command** widget is iPad-only. Apple Watch pairs with iPhone, so the watch app and complication get their data from your iPhone.

## Common questions

### How do I back up my practices?

Open the menu → **Data & Storage** → **Export JSON**. You'll get a full backup file you can save to Files, email to yourself, or AirDrop. **Export to Excel** gives you a spreadsheet with your completion log.

To restore later: same menu → **Restore Backup** → pick the JSON file. Restoring replaces all current data, so export first if there's anything you want to keep. **Import from File** is different: it merges practices from a CSV, spreadsheet, or JSON file, with a review step.

If you use more than one device, [iCloud sync](#icloud-sync) keeps a copy in your iCloud too. It's still worth exporting a backup now and then.

### What happened to Lumen?

Lumen, the optional on-device assistant in Praxis 1.5 and earlier, has been retired in Praxis 2.0. The insight cards stay, and they're free: find them in **Stats → Insights**. If you had downloaded Lumen's model, updating to 2.0 deletes it and gives you back about 1.9 GB of storage, and Praxis shows a short note once to say so. Your practices, history and notes aren't touched.

If you're still on Praxis 1.5, for example on a device that can't update to iOS 17, Lumen keeps working there as before. Lumen is built with Llama. Llama 3.2 is licensed under the Llama 3.2 Community License, Copyright © Meta Platforms, Inc. All Rights Reserved. [Read the Llama 3.2 Community License ↗](https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_2/LICENSE)

### Can I share practices with my household?

Not yet. Sharing practices with the people you live with is coming in a future update.

### What's free, and what does the unlock include?

Praxis is free to download, and the free version isn't a trial. It includes up to **5 practices, 2 life areas, 4 rooms, 2 zones, and 1 Flow**, plus widgets, the Apple Watch app, Live Activities, insight cards and backups. Sample data doesn't count toward those limits.

A **one-time purchase** ($9.99 in the US; the price varies by country) unlocks:

- Unlimited practices, areas, rooms, zones, and Flows
- **iCloud sync** between your devices
- Editing the sample practices

No subscription, no recurring charges. Open the menu → **Unlock Praxis**. If you already had more than the free limits before they were introduced, you keep everything; the limits only apply when adding more.

### How do I restore a previous purchase?

Open the menu → **Unlock Praxis** → **Restore previous purchase**. Make sure you're signed in with the same Apple ID that bought Praxis originally. Praxis also checks with the App Store every time it launches, so this usually happens on its own. If **Unlock Praxis** isn't in the menu, you're already unlocked.

### How do I change or turn off the daily notification?

Open the menu → **Settings** → **Notifications**. Turn **Daily check-in** off, or change its **Reminder time**. Reminders for individual practices are in each practice's details, under **Reminder**.

### How do I delete a practice or a logged session?

**Practice:** in the **Practices** tab, swipe the practice left and tap **Delete**. Or open it and tap **Delete Practice** at the bottom.

**Logged session:** in the **Practices** tab, tap the practice, switch to its **History** tab, and tap **✕** next to the entry. Tap **Edit** instead to fix a date, time, duration, or count. **+ Add Entry** logs a past session by hand.

With iCloud sync on, deletions carry over to your other devices.

## Common workflows

A few patterns that come up over and over. Each is a way to get more out of features Praxis already has.

### Build a morning routine

1. Add the individual practices first (*Meditate, Journal, Workout, Shower*), each as its own practice.
2. Open the menu → **Flows** → **+**.
3. Name the Flow *Morning*. Add the practices in order. Save.
4. Tap the Flow tomorrow morning. Each practice leads into the next, with no decisions in between.

The reason this works better than a single "Morning Routine" practice: you still get separate cadence, scoring, and history for each piece. *Workout* is on its own three-day cycle whether or not you did the rest of the routine that day.

### Track something that doesn't fit a fixed schedule

Some practices aren't daily or weekly. They're "when it makes sense." *Call Mom. Deep clean the bathroom. Read fiction.*

Set the **target** to roughly how often you'd like to (every 7 days, every 14, every 30) and **max** to your patience level (when does it start to feel neglected?). Don't worry about getting it exact. Adjust the numbers later if scoring doesn't match how you actually feel about the practice.

### Keep a weekday practice from nagging on weekends

Open the practice, find **Applies on**, and pick the days it counts. On other days it moves to **Not today** on Focus and doesn't become overdue.

### Pair related practices with a link

Some pairs naturally belong together: one prepares for the other, or two of them shouldn't share a day. Open the practice that should depend on another, scroll to **Links**, and add the link.

Common ones:
- *Stretch → Run, within 1 day.*
- *Cardio → Cardio, 2 days minimum.*
- *Plan the week → Execute the week, within 1 day.*

Most practices don't need a link. Use them when the dependency is obvious.

### See what needs you, and why

Focus already puts the practices that need you most at the top, the most overdue first within each group.

If you want to know *why* a practice keeps running past its target (was it always this way, or did it change recently?), swipe it on Focus → **Stats** and look at the gaps between sessions over time. The pattern is usually obvious once you can see the timeline. If the target itself no longer fits your life, change it; an insight card may already have suggested one.

### Plan a realistic week

Open the **Plan** tab. Praxis suggests practices for each day of the upcoming week based on cadence, what's overdue, and the days each practice applies. If two linked practices land in a way that breaks a link, the app shows a swap indicator so you can rebalance.

Plan is most useful when you've got 10+ practices going. With fewer than that, Focus alone is usually enough.

## Bugs and feedback

If something doesn't work right, or you have an idea for what Praxis should do better, email **praxis.app.support@gmail.com**. Include:

- What you were trying to do
- What happened instead
- Your device model and iOS version (Settings → General → About), and your watchOS version if it's about the watch
- Whether iCloud sync is turned on, if it's about sync

Screenshots help.

## Refunds

Refunds for the Praxis in-app purchase are handled by Apple, not by me. Open the [Apple "Report a Problem"](https://reportaproblem.apple.com/) page, sign in, find Praxis in your purchase history, and request a refund. Apple makes the decision; I don't have visibility into individual transactions.

## Privacy

See the full [Privacy Policy](privacy) for details on what Praxis stores and where. Short version: there's no Praxis account and no Praxis server. Your practices stay on your devices, and in your own iCloud if you turn on sync. Crash and freeze reports go to Sentry to help fix bugs, and they never include your practice content. You can turn them off in Praxis: open the menu, tap **Settings**, and turn off **Send crash reports** under **Privacy**.

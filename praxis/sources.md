# Sources

The quotes and frequency recommendations in Praxis are drawn from real classical texts and real research. This page lists the primary sources and links to each one.

## Why this page exists

Praxis is built on the idea that small repeated actions matter. The same standard applies to what the app says: every claim should be sourceable, every classical quote correctly attributed, every recommendation traceable to real evidence.

We cite real institutions (FDA, CDC, WHO, NSF, ADA, AAD), real peer-reviewed research, and real classical texts. Where the popular version of a quote turned out to be apocryphal — wrongly attributed to Aristotle, Marcus Aurelius, the Buddha, or Confucius — we replaced it with a sourced alternative. Where an empirical claim couldn't be verified, we either found a better source or rephrased it more honestly.

If you find an error here, please email **praxis.app.support@gmail.com**.

## Classical sources

### Aristotle

The name *Praxis* and much of the app's framing comes from Aristotle's distinction between three modes of activity: *theoria* (contemplation), *poiesis* (making for an outcome), and *praxis* (action whose end is the action itself).

- **The praxis / poiesis distinction** — Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book VI, Chapters 2–4. [Internet Classics Archive (W. D. Ross translation)](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.html)
- **"Moral excellence comes about as a result of habit"** — Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* II.1. [Read on Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)
- **"Well begun is half done"** — quoted as a proverb by Aristotle in *Politics* V.4 (1303b29). [Read on Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/politics.5.five.html)

### Will Durant (paraphrasing Aristotle)

- **"We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit."** — Will Durant, *The Story of Philosophy* (1926), p. 76. This is Durant's paraphrase of Aristotle's concept of *hexis* (habit / disposition), not Aristotle's exact words. [Read on Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/storyofphilosoph00dura)

### Seneca

- **"Begin at once to live, and count each separate day as a separate life."** — Seneca, *Moral Letters to Lucilius*, Letter 101.10. [Read on Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_101)
- **"It is not that things are difficult that we do not dare; it is because we do not dare that they are difficult."** — Seneca, *Letters*, Letter 104. [Read on Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius)
- **"While we hesitate, life passes."** — Seneca, *Letters*, Letter 1.2 (*"Dum differtur vita transcurrit"*). [Read on Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_1)

### Epictetus

- **"First say to yourself what you would be; then do what you have to do."** — *Discourses* 3.23.1. [Read on Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/discourses.html)
- **"Make the best use of what is in your power, and take the rest as it happens."** — *Discourses* 1.1.17 and *Enchiridion* 1. [Read on Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/discourses.1.one.html)
- **"No man is free who is not master of himself."** — *Discourses* 4.1.

### Marcus Aurelius

- **"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."** — *Meditations* 5.20 (Hays translation). [Read on Internet Classics Archive (Long translation)](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)
- **"Waste no more time arguing about what a good person should be. Be one."** — *Meditations* 10.16. [Read on Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.10.ten.html)
- **"Confine yourself to the present."** — *Meditations* 7.29. [Read on Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.7.seven.html)
- **"Take away the complaint, 'I have been harmed,' and the harm is taken away."** — *Meditations* 4.7.

### Lao Tzu

- **"A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step."** — *Tao Te Ching*, Chapter 64 (千里之行，始於足下). [Read on Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/dao-de-jing)

### The Buddha (Pali Canon)

- **"What is past is left behind. The future is as yet unreached. Whatever quality is present, see it clearly there."** — *Bhaddekaratta Sutta*, Majjhima Nikaya 131. [Read on Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.131.than.html)

### Confucius

- **"Learning without thinking is labor lost."** — *Analects* 2.15.

## Research sources for app frequency recommendations

### Wellness

- **Mindfulness and brain gray matter** — Hölzel et al. (2011), *"Mindfulness practice leads to increases in regional brain gray matter density,"* Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging, 191(1), 36–43. [Read on PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3004979/)
- **Physical activity and cardiovascular health** — World Health Organization, [Physical Activity Fact Sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity). The 150 min/week recommendation is verbatim WHO; the ~35% reduction in CVD mortality is from broader meta-analytic evidence cited in WHO's Physical Activity Guidelines.
- **Cold exposure** — Søberg et al. (2021), *"Altered brown fat thermoregulation and enhanced cold-induced thermogenesis in young, healthy, winter-swimming men,"* Cell Reports Medicine, 2(10). [Read on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34755128/)
- **Bedtime routines** — National Sleep Foundation, [How to Build a Better Bedtime Routine for Adults](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/bedtime-routine-for-adults).
- **Flexibility training** — American College of Sports Medicine, [Physical Activity Guidelines](https://acsm.org/education-resources/trending-topics-resources/physical-activity-guidelines/). ACSM recommends ≥2-3 days per week of stretching, with greater gains attainable from daily practice.

### Mental and productivity

- **Expressive writing for stress and immunity** — Pennebaker (1997), *"Writing about emotional experiences as a therapeutic process,"* Psychological Science 8(3), 162–166. [SAGE abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1997.tb00403.x)
- **Reading and longevity** — Bavishi, Slade & Levy (2016), *"A chapter a day: Association of book reading with longevity,"* Social Science & Medicine 164, 44–48. [Read on PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5105607/)
- **Spaced practice** — Hermann Ebbinghaus, *Über das Gedächtnis* (1885) — original spacing-effect research. [Full text on Psychology Classics](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Ebbinghaus/index.htm)
- **Deliberate practice** — Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993), *"The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance,"* Psychological Review 100(3), 363–406. [APA PsycNet](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1993-40718-001)
- **Weekly review for productivity** — David Allen, *Getting Things Done: The Art of Stress-Free Productivity* (2001/2015). [Official GTD site](https://gettingthingsdone.com/)
- **Planning fallacy** — Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994), *"Exploring the 'planning fallacy': Why people underestimate their task completion times,"* Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 67(3), 366–381. The "Most things take less time than you think" framing in onboarding draws on this and on Kahneman & Tversky's earlier work on intuitive prediction.

### Relationships

- **Close relationships and longevity** — Harvard Study of Adult Development (1939–present), the longest-running study of adult life. [Official site](https://www.adultdevelopmentstudy.org/) · Robert Waldinger and Marc Schulz, *The Good Life* (2023).
- **Friendship maintenance frequency** — Robin Dunbar (Oxford), research on social network layers. The 5-layer "closest friends" tier requires roughly weekly contact. See Dunbar's [research page](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/people/robin.dunbar).
- **Couple relationships and rituals of connection** — The Gottman Institute. [Official site](https://www.gottman.com/) · John Gottman & Nan Silver, *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*. Gottman's research recommends daily 20-minute connection rituals and weekly date nights.

### Cleaning, health, and household

- **Clutter and stress** — Saxbe & Repetti (2010), *"No Place Like Home: Home Tours Correlate With Daily Patterns of Mood and Cortisol,"* Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 36(1), 71–81. [SAGE abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167209352864) · UCLA Center on Everyday Lives of Families (CELF). [CELF homepage](https://celf.anthro.ucla.edu/)
- **Food safety surface cleaning** — FDA Food Code 2022, Chapter 4. [FDA Food Code page](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022). The 4-hour rule for food-contact surfaces is in §4-602.11.
- **Household germ contamination** — NSF International Household Germ Studies. [Germiest items in the home](https://www.nsf.org/consumer-resources/articles/clean-germiest-home-items)
- **Allergens and dust mites** — Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America. [Dust Mite Allergy](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/insect-allergy/dust-mite-allergy/) — recommends weekly vacuuming with a HEPA filter.
- **Mold and respiratory health** — Centers for Disease Control. [Mold and Your Health](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html)
- **Cooking fires** — National Fire Protection Association. [Home Cooking Fires Report](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/research/nfpa-research/fire-statistical-reports/home-cooking-fires) — cooking is the leading cause of US home fires (49%), with grease/oil/fat the leading ignition material (45%).
- **Laundry hygiene** — American Cleaning Institute. [Laundry Basics](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-tips/clothes/laundry-basics)

### Personal care

- **Flossing and gum health** — American Dental Association. [Floss page](https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/floss). The ADA recommends daily interdental cleaning. The underlying evidence (per Cochrane systematic reviews) shows a small but statistically significant short-term reduction in gingivitis when flossing is added to brushing.
- **Skincare** — American Academy of Dermatology. [Skin Care Basics](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics)
- **Dental visits** — American Dental Association. [Dental Visits](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/dental-visits)

### Fasting

- **Intermittent fasting and metabolic health** — de Cabo & Mattson (2019), *"Effects of Intermittent Fasting on Health, Aging, and Disease,"* New England Journal of Medicine 381, 2541–2551. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31881139/) — The review describes the metabolic switching effects of extended fasting windows (the canonical example in the paper is 18:6, but findings generalize to 16-hour daily fasts as well).
- **Autophagy and extended fasts** — Longo & Mattson (2014), *"Fasting: Molecular Mechanisms and Clinical Applications,"* Cell Metabolism 19(2), 181–192. [Read on PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946160/)

### Finance

- **Expense tracking and overspending** — Meta-analyses of self-control strategies in personal finance show daily expense tracking reduces monthly spending by approximately $228–$236 median. See [University of Wisconsin working paper on expense tracking](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/63XZYPDLKMY638S/R/file-908a3.pdf).
- **Budget review** — Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. [Budgeting guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/want-make-your-money-grow-faster-budget/)

### Stoic concepts in Lumen's prompt

The on-device AI inside Praxis (Lumen) is built around several Stoic concepts:

- **Prosochē** (προσοχή — attention, mindfulness) — Epictetus, *Discourses* throughout.
- **Askēsis** (ἄσκησις — practice, training) — see Pierre Hadot, *Philosophy as a Way of Life* (1995) for the canonical modern treatment.
- **Dichotomy of control** — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1.1.
- ***Amor fati*** (love of fate) — Friedrich Nietzsche, *Ecce Homo* and *The Gay Science* §276. *Amor fati* is technically Nietzsche's concept rather than a classical Stoic one, though it sits in the same family of acceptance-of-reality philosophies.

## A note on the apocryphal

A few quotes circulating online with famous names attached turn out to be apocryphal — never said by the person they're attributed to. Examples we deliberately avoid in Praxis:

- *"It does not matter how slowly you go as long as you do not stop"* — widely attributed to Confucius but not in the *Analects*. Origin unknown.
- *"Do not dwell in the past..."* — attributed to the Buddha but not in the Pali Canon. Replaced in our app with the actual Bhaddekaratta Sutta passage cited above.
- *"You have power over your mind, not outside events..."* — attributed to Marcus Aurelius but not in the *Meditations*. A modern paraphrase.
- The *"reading 6 minutes reduces stress 68%"* statistic was a 2009 marketing-funded press release by Mindlab International, not a peer-reviewed study. Replaced in Praxis with the Bavishi et al. 2016 longevity study on book reading.

If you spot something you believe is misattributed or unsourced, please write to **praxis.app.support@gmail.com**.
